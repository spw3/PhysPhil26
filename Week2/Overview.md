Week 2: Stoicism, Determinism, and the birth of Modern Physics

Recommended readings:
- Stoicism and Free Will: What Do Stoics Believe About Choices? (see files in this folder)
- The determinist challenge to free will (see files in this folder)

Extended reading (recommended):
- The Dome Paradox: A Loophole in Newton's Laws (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EjZB81jCGj4)
- The Dome: A Simple Violation of Determinism in Newtonian Mechanics (https://sites.pitt.edu/~jdnorton/Goodies/Dome/#Note%206%C2%A0)

Guiding Questions: 
- How does the article define free will? Does it use the term consistently?
- How did the Stoics reconcile determinism with human responsibility?
- Before modern physics, how persuasive could such a deterministic worldview be without a mathematical framework?

Discussion Points:
- Does it make sense for us to extend Newtonian physics to the rest of reality? In general, should we assume our working model of the universe is generalisable?
- Arguments against Norton's Dome defense, such as being very contrived and impossible to achieve the "symmetric instability"
- Does an indeterministic Newtonian system even rescue free will or just leave us in Laplace's chance objection?
